# What NOT to write in your friend’s wedding guestbook

<p class="article-meta">{{ page.date | date: '%-d %B %Y' }}</p>

When it is your friend’s wedding day, all the celebrations can make it difficult to catch up one-on-one. A guestbook is the perfect opportunity to leave the couple a heartfelt and special message that they can look back on for years to come.

But let’s be honest: after a few drinks, you might think it is funny to leave a unique, hilarious message for the bride and groom to read later. Be careful what you write—you may regret it in the morning.

Here are some messages you should **not** write in your friend’s wedding guestbook, so the couple can enjoy their day and you can avoid embarrassing yourself.

![Wedding guestbook inspiration]({{ '/assets/images/what-not-to-write-in-guestbook.jpg' | relative_url }})

## Jokes about divorce

Sometimes it is funny to make jokes about how much your partner annoys you, but save these for face-to-face conversations where you can read your audience’s reactions. The guestbook is not the place. Your friends may be together forever, but the joke might not age well. Stick to congratulations and tell the couple how happy you are to share their big day.

![A bride in a moment of frustration]({{ '/assets/images/jokes-about-divorce.jpg' | relative_url }})

## That you are going to book the same venue

You may love the place where your friend or family member is hosting their wedding, whether it is a beautiful traditional castle or a sandy beach. But your loved one probably does not want you to have the same wedding. You can tell them how much you enjoyed their day; just do not use the guestbook to announce that you are considering the same venue. If their lighting inspired you, find your own way to use a bold feature, such as [Love Letters]({{ '/love-letters/' | relative_url }}).

![An awkward wedding moment]({{ '/assets/images/awkward-moment-bride.jpg' | relative_url }})

## Secrets about the couple

If you are good friends with the bride or groom, you probably know some secrets about them. Remember that the guestbook may be read by other people, and sharing a secret could upset a new husband or wife or embarrass the couple. Save private stories for another time. If you want to bring one up, speak to your friend privately instead.

![An unhappy wedding couple]({{ '/assets/images/wedding-couple-unhappy.jpg' | relative_url }})

## That they have spent too much money

Some people dream of an extravagant wedding; others prefer a small, traditional ceremony. Everyone is different. Telling the bride and groom your opinion of what they spent will not make them feel good. Keep your note positive instead—mention how much you enjoyed the dinner or the [Garden Games]({{ '/garden-games/' | relative_url }}).

![A wedding photographer at work]({{ '/assets/images/photograher.jpg' | relative_url }})

## You did not like the photographer

Photos are an important part of a wedding. You may not like the style of the photographs or how often the photographer comes around, but the guestbook is not the place for criticism. ## Asking when they are having children

Once people are married, they are often asked when they are having a child. But not everyone wants children, and family plans are personal. Avoid adding pressure or assumptions on the wedding day; that conversation can wait.
